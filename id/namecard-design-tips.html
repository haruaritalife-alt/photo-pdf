Tips Membuat Kartu Nama yang Mudah Dibaca: Ukuran Teks, Warna, dan Ruang

Diterbitkan: 19 Okt 2026 | Diperbarui: 19 Okt 2026 | Tentang (bahasa Jepang)

Poin penting
  • Jadikan nama sebagai teks terbesar
  • Tetap pada satu warna
  • Jangan menjejalkan ruang

Even with the same information, design changes readability a great deal. Using the options in Create Business Cards / Name Cards, here are tips for a readable business card.

1. Make the name stand out most

The most important thing on a card is the name. Keeping the affiliation and title more restrained than the name balances it.

2. Do not over-choose colours

"Color" has teal, navy, red, green, purple and black.

  • Navy, black: a calm impression.
  • Teal, green: a soft impression.
  • Red: a striking impression. Limit the area of strong colours.
  • Purple: a distinctive impression.

3. Choose a typeface

  • Sans-serif: readable and modern.
  • Serif: elegant and calm.

Pick one and keep it consistent across the card.

4. Choose an accent

  • Top band: a coloured band at the top tightens the card.
  • Left stripe: a subtle divider.
  • None: simple, and saves ink.

5. Text alignment

  • Left: a tidy impression.
  • Centre: well balanced, suits name tags.

6. Leave margins

Do not cram text in; keep contacts to 3 lines at most. Margins improve readability.

Langkah-langkah

  1. Enter the contents.
  2. Under "Look", choose colour, typeface, accent and alignment.
  3. Check the preview with "Make cards".
  4. If something bothers you, choose again and remake it.
The impression on screen can differ from the colour when printed. Confirm with a test print.

Examples

A student's card: navy, sans-serif, top band, left-aligned. An event name tag: teal, sans-serif, none, centred.

Common stumbling blocks

  • The text is hard to read: choose a darker colour.
  • The look is cluttered: set the accent to "None".
  • The colour is different: it looks different on different printers.

Ringkasan

Make the name stand out, limit colour and typeface, and leave margins. These three make a card readable.
